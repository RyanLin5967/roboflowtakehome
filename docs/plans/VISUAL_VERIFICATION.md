# Original-image controls and three-valued verification

New live recipes use `counterpart.recipe/2`, recipe `2.0.0`, and verifier `counterpart-verifier/2.0.0`. Existing recipe `1.0.0` snapshots and illustrative replay retain their original rules and serialized decisions. A contract is rebuilt from its exact registered version and compared in full, not merely checked against a client-recomputed digest. Runtime policy changes fail closed rather than silently reinterpreting saved sessions.

The v2 recipe composes named geometry, background registration, observed appearance continuity, and up to three additional protected object classes. Protected classes use the existing supported detector vocabulary, must differ from both roles and from one another, and are frozen into the recipe. This is a bounded recipe family with explicit clauses, not an arbitrary executable rule language.

Each clause returns `pass`, `fail`, or `unknown`; the decision returns the same three values. `accepted` is true only when every required clause passes. Known failures dominate unknowns. Structural corruption has `hard_error: true` and is not reviewable. An ambiguous object cannot hide independent evidence that the reference moved, another object's lane changed, or the recording contains an invalid temporal gap. The historical verifier remains unchanged; v2 additionally evaluates independent controls even when a first detector ambiguity prevents the historical complete check.

An operator may separately authorize continued capture from an unknown v2 baseline only when the named role geometry already passes strictly, declared protected objects are visible and geometrically consistent, and the unresolved clauses concern visual registration or appearance. Missing detections, failed controls, corrupted evidence and absent original images cannot be waived. The authorization binds the exact recipe, original decision and original frame records; the authenticated durable record also binds session, capture and execution identity. Verification consumes this authority separately without changing the original `accepted: false`. All counterpart geometry and pixel controls still run, and the resulting pair requires explicit human adjudication rather than ordinary machine-only publication. An authorization is not a global waiver of future unknowns.

## Pixel evidence and bounded computation

The worker supplies its own immutable-media reader. The visual verifier reads original bytes, verifies their SHA-256 against their asset ID and byte count, checks image dimensions and decoding bounds, then computes its own features. Client-submitted transforms, crops, appearance descriptors, and checks are never trusted. Visual evidence records original reference/candidate hashes and both original-pixel and normalized candidate-to-reference transforms.

Every saved temporal frame is checked against the first original baseline image. A first baseline also needs enough distributed texture to support future registration; a flat image is unknown, not proof that the camera is fixed. Original files are at most four megapixels, images are downsampled to at most 640 pixels per side, and at most 900 ORB features are extracted. All plausible detected object boxes, including additional detections outside the named roles, are expanded and excluded from background features. Native OpenCV uses one thread and OpenCL is disabled. Up to 48 current frames are processed sequentially, retaining the reference and current decoded frame rather than a full decoded video.

Reciprocal ratio-filtered Hamming matches feed a seeded, bounded 500-iteration RANSAC 2-D similarity fit. Acceptance requires at least 14 inliers, an inlier fraction of 0.65, support across at least four of nine image cells, support area of 0.12 in both images, and bounded fit residual. Registration is insufficient when correspondences are sparse, ambiguous, or concentrated. Estimated corner displacement, rotation and scale are checked against the saved camera tolerance. A transform does not compensate away camera movement and turn it into a pass.

For declared protected objects, registered positions and apparent sizes must stay stable. Missing, ambiguous, tiny or clipped detections produce unknown. Role and protected-object crops undergo a bounded RGB-histogram and normalized-patch comparison. The evidence describes **appearance continuity**, not physical identity. Visually identical replacements can pass, and lighting or viewpoint changes can be inconclusive or violate the appearance control. Registration estimates a 2-D background transform; it cannot prove 3-D camera pose, lighting constancy, every undeclared object remaining unchanged, or detector truth.

## Verification evidence

`tests/test_visual_v2.py` covers positive full-pair decisions, exact byte/transform binding, deterministic re-evaluation, concurrent v1/v2 execution, frozen policies, canonical protected classes, missing-byte refusal and unknown-versus-known-failure composition. Independent attacks in `tests/test_visual_attack.py` cover translated/mirrored/zoomed/sheared/unrelated backgrounds, moving-only designated objects, limited texture, concentrated support, duplicate roles, disappearing/moving protected objects, appearance changes, an invalid middle video frame, and malformed/hash-inconsistent observations.

These use generated pixels with declared observations. They test implemented image algorithms and evidence handling, not a detector benchmark or real physical collection accuracy. The user explicitly deferred the camera trial; thresholds remain engineering defaults pending that study.

## Reused implementation

OpenCV supplies the conventional [ORB feature detector and descriptors](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d1/d89/tutorial_py_orb.html) and [robust 2-D transformation estimation](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d9/d0c/group__calib3d.html). The project does not claim to invent these algorithms. `opencv-python-headless` is pinned in the lockfile; no trained model or local ML inference is used for these checks. Hosted Roboflow detections remain the perception source for live observations.
